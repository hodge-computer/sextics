# Worker C — Eight-component compact solver

**Target:** Solve the eight-component rational differential system for `cycle-q2`, retaining the complete five-component feedback from Worker B.

Write the 13-dimensional connection in block form:

\[
\Gamma_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_i&\Theta_i\\
0&B_i
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad i\in\{P,U\},
\]

where \(A_i\) is \(8\times8\), \(B_i\) is \(5\times5\), and \(\Theta_i\) is \(8\times5\).

The exact compact target is

\[
\boxed{
\nabla_P^{(8)}\mathbf a_P+
\nabla_U^{(8)}\mathbf a_U
=\mathbf h
}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf h=\mathbf r_8-\Theta_P\mathbf b_P-\Theta_U\mathbf b_U,
\qquad
\nabla_i^{(8)}=\partial_i+A_i.
}
\]

The supplied research currently establishes this decomposition and finite-field specializations, but not the generic rational matrices or a rational solution.

The essential compatibility condition is

\[
\partial_P A_U-\partial_U A_P+[A_P,A_U]=0.
\]

This guarantees that \(U\)-differentiation respects equivalence modulo \(P\)-exact terms.

For a fixed admissible pole divisor, let \(\mathcal M\) denote the allowed rational eight-vectors and form

\[
\mathcal H_P=\mathcal M/\nabla_P^{(8)}\mathcal M.
\]

If we construct a finite reduction basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\), with explicit exact witnesses, then

\[
[\mathbf h]=\sum_j g_j(U)[e_j],
\]

and

\[
[\nabla_U^{(8)}e_j]
=\sum_k C_{kj}(U)[e_k].
\]

The two-variable problem reduces to the one-variable rational system

\[
\boxed{\mathbf z'(U)+C(U)\mathbf z(U)=\mathbf g(U).}
\]

Once a rational \(\mathbf z\) exists, the recorded \(P\)-exact witnesses reconstruct \(\mathbf a_P\) exactly.

**Scope:** A finite reduction basis requires a justified pole-support restriction. With arbitrary new rational poles, the unrestricted quotient need not be finite-dimensional. Thus this is an exact integration method within a specified rational differential module, not an unproved universal completeness theorem.

## 2. Exact moving-pole integration

A useful new component is the treatment of **moving poles without freezing their parameter dependence**.

For a pole divisor \(t=f(P,U)=0\), set \(u=U\), \(q=f_P\), and \(s=f_U\). The correct transformed compact currents are

\[
a^t=a_P+\frac{s}{q}a_U,\qquad a^u=\frac{a_U}{q}.
\]

The connection transforms as

\[
A_t=\frac{A_P}{q},\qquad
A_u^{\mathrm{adapt}}=A_U-\frac{s}{q}A_P,
\]

and the source becomes \(h/q\). I verified symbolically that this transformation preserves the complete covariant divergence, including all Jacobian derivatives.

At a regular-singular moving pole, suppose

\[
A_t=\frac{R(u)}t+O(1),\qquad
A_u^{\mathrm{adapt}}=S(u)+O(t).
\]

Then flatness imposes

\[
R'+[S,R]=0.
\]

For the simple-pole channel, the rational integration problem descends to a differential equation on the cokernel of \(R\). With \(L R=0\) and \(LV=I\), the induced connection is

\[
\boxed{
C=(LS-L')V,\qquad
y'+Cy=Lh_{-1}.
}
\]

This is particularly useful because it retains the moving residue basis through the \(L'\) term. Positive-integer resonances can introduce further Laurent channels and must be checked separately.

## 3. Worker B feedback is integrated

A further exact identity handles Worker B's freedom to change its five-current by a covariant curl. For any rational five-vector \(\tau\),

\[
\delta b_P=\nabla_U^{(5)}\tau,\qquad
\delta b_U=-\nabla_P^{(5)}\tau.
\]

The compact currents can compensate through

\[
\boxed{
\delta a_P=\Theta_U\tau,\qquad
\delta a_U=-\Theta_P\tau.
}
\]

The full 13-dimensional flatness identity proves that these transformations leave the complete divergence unchanged.

This means Worker B can supply a convenient rational representative without forcing Worker C to restart. However, Worker B should also preserve **non-exact homogeneous residue-current classes**, since those may genuinely alter the compact obstruction.
