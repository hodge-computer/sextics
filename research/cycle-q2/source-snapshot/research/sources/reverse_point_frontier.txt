### Why this proves finiteness

The coherent quotient \(I/J\) has closed support in the projective base \(B\), so that support is proper. Boundary equality places the support entirely inside the affine torus. A scheme that is both affine and proper over the ground field is finite. Thus
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Supp}(I/J)\text{ is finite}.}
\]
The last implication is the standard affine-plus-proper criterion for finiteness. ([stacks.math.columbia.edu](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01WN?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Because the entire center is also accounted for, this finite support is disjoint from \(V(I)\). Define the colon ideal
\[
K_{\mathrm{def}}=J:I.
\]
We then have actual global sheaf identities
\[
\boxed{
I+K_{\mathrm{def}}=\mathcal O,\qquad
J=I K_{\mathrm{def}}=I\cap K_{\mathrm{def}},\qquad
I/J\simeq\mathcal O/K_{\mathrm{def}}.
}
\]

Consequently, the adjoint cohomology is now expressed as
\[
\boxed{
H^1\!\left(B,J(40,64,40)\right)
=
\operatorname{coker}\!\left[
H^0\!\left(B,I(40,64,40)\right)
\longrightarrow
H^0\!\left(B,(\mathcal O/K_{\mathrm{def}})(40,64,40)\right)
\right].
}
\]

The domain is already known:

|---|---:|
| Full \(H^0(B,I(40,64,40))\) | \(16{,}302\) |
| Retained source-compatible character block | \(2{,}706\) |
| Presentation of that block | \(2{,}742\) columns modulo \(36\) relations |

These are dimensions of the **domain of the evaluation map**. The target’s length and the evaluation rank are still unknown.

After splitting the finite points, the target is a direct sum of local Artin quotient rings. The remaining calculation must determine those actual rings, including their multiplicities and character action. Finiteness alone does not supply their defining ideals or justify a chosen jet-truncation depth. The defect could also be empty; no nonemptiness conclusion has been proved.
