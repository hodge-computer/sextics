## A stronger connection: the elliptic machinery is actually shared

Comparing the exact matrices in the project files reveals something more specific than the general similarity.

Both constructions use the **same universal rank-2 elliptic Gauss–Manin connection**, expressed in different bases.

For reflection-coupled-residual, the elliptic quartic has invariants \(I,J\). Its Weierstrass model is

\[
Y^2=X^3-\frac I3X-\frac J{27}.
\]

Cycle-q2 uses the general Weierstrass curve

\[
y^2=x^3+A_ex+B_e.
\]

Make the identification

\[
A_e=-\frac I3,\qquad B_e=-\frac J{27},
\]

and define

\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1/24\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then the connections satisfy the exact identities

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_I^{\mathrm{ref}}
&=T^{-1}\left(-\frac13M_{A_e}^{\mathrm{cycle}}\right)^{\mathsf T}T,\\
\Gamma_J^{\mathrm{ref}}
&=T^{-1}\left(-\frac1{27}M_{B_e}^{\mathrm{cycle}}\right)^{\mathsf T}T.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

I checked both identities symbolically over \(\mathbb Q(I,J)\); every matrix entry cancels exactly.

The transpose accounts for the different basis/coefficient conventions. The constant diagonal matrix comes from the relationship between their elliptic differential bases.

**That means a universal solver for this elliptic differential module could be reused directly across the two problems.** We would still need separate source adapters and residue-current coupling, but we would not need to independently develop the elliptic integration machinery.
