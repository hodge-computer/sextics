DETERMINANT-FREE PRESENTATIONS OF THE JACOBIAN OPEN

Scope and notation
------------------
Let K be a field, f=(f_1,...,f_n) in K[x_1,...,x_n]^n, and
J=(partial f_i/partial x_j), D=det J.  Additional prescribed polynomial
opens may be imposed throughout.  Write A for their localization of
K[x]/(f).  No reducedness, equidimensionality, complete-intersection
regularity, or finiteness assumption is imposed on A.

The conclusions below are algebraic.  The dimension proof is not formalized
in Lean.  The accompanying scripts exactly audit the actual contact-system
derivatives and emit sparse solver inputs; they do not claim a completed
quotient, a trace value, or a final certificate.

1. Two independent generic right-hand sides impose exactly D!=0
--------------------------------------------------------------
Let b_1,...,b_n,c_1,...,c_n be algebraically independent over K and put
L=K(b_1,...,b_n,c_1,...,c_n).  Define

  B=L[x,v,w]/(f(x), J(x)v-b, J(x)w-c),

with the same prescribed opens as A.  Then D is a unit in B, and

  B is canonically isomorphic to A[D^{-1}] tensor_K L.

Proof of the generic exclusion.  Work first over an algebraic closure of K.
For delta=1,...,n, let S_delta be the locally closed rank stratum in the
underlying reduced zero set of f on which rank J=n-delta.  At any closed
point of this stratum its tangent space is contained in ker J.  The local
dimension is bounded by tangent-space dimension, so dim S_delta<=delta.
This remains true when the original equations define a nonreduced scheme:
the tangent space of the reduced stratum is still contained in the kernel
of the derivatives of the original equations.

Over S_delta the images im J form a vector bundle of rank n-delta.  The
incidence of triples (x,b,c) with b,c in im J_x therefore has dimension

  dim S_delta + 2(n-delta) <= 2n-delta < 2n.

Its image in the affine 2n-dimensional RHS parameter space has proper
Zariski closure.  The finite union of these closures is still proper.
The independent generic point (b,c) avoids it.  Consequently the algebra B
has no geometric point with D=0.  By the Nullstellensatz and faithful field
extension, the ideal obtained by adding D to its presentation is the unit
ideal.  Thus D is a unit in B, scheme-theoretically, not merely on a chosen
reduced component.

Proof of the isomorphism.  On D!=0 the vectors are uniquely
v=adj(J)b/D and w=adj(J)c/D.  These expressions define the inverse to the
map A[D^{-1}] tensor L -> B.  This proves the claimed algebra isomorphism,
including any possible nilpotent structure.  The Jacobian criterion then
shows that this algebra is etale over L.  A finite-type etale algebra over
a field is a finite product of finite separable field extensions (or the
zero algebra).  In particular it is finite and reduced.

Characteristic qualifications.  The geometric dimension argument and the
invertible-Jacobian conclusion work over an arbitrary field after passage
to an algebraic closure.  Characteristic zero is the relevant setting of
the contact problem, but is not needed for this generic-exclusion lemma.
Over an infinite field there are fixed RHS choices in a nonempty Zariski
open with the same property.  Over a finite field that open need not have
a rational point.  Neither statement proves that a displayed deterministic
modular RHS pair is good.  No such claim is made for the emitted vectors.

2. A completed zero-dimensional fixed-RHS quotient certifies itself
------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix any b, or any b,c, over the exact field being used.  Keep all auxiliary
solve coordinates unrestricted.  If the resulting augmented algebra is
proved zero-dimensional, then D is necessarily a unit in it, and it is
canonically isomorphic to A[D^{-1}].  This applies to ONE RHS as well as two.

Indeed, a surviving geometric root x with corank delta>0 would have an
affine delta-dimensional fiber of solutions to Jv=b, or an affine
2delta-dimensional fiber for two vectors.  Such a fiber is a closed
quotient of the base-changed augmented algebra.  A zero-dimensional
finite-type algebra cannot have a positive-dimensional quotient.  Thus
there are no singular geometric roots; D is a unit, and the same explicit
adjugate isomorphism applies.  This also rules out nilpotents, since the
result is etale.

Consequently an independently verified completed zero-dimensional
Groebner/border-basis certificate is a sufficient acceptance gate for the
fixed RHS inputs.  A timeout, an incomplete basis, finite point samples,
or merely the expectation of generic RHS values is not such a gate.
In particular, a finite-field certificate concerns that finite-field
fiber; it does not by itself prove generic characteristic-zero flatness
or any of the nine target trace identities.

3. Why one generic RHS has no universal guarantee
------------------------------------------------
For f=(x,x*y), all original roots x=0 are singular and

  J=[[1,0],[y,x]].

Over K(b1,b2), the system Jv=b has the solutions

  x=0, y=b2/b1, v1=b1, v2 arbitrary.

Thus the one-RHS augmented algebra is a polynomial ring in v2, although
the desired D-open is empty.  With two independent RHS vectors, the
additional consistency relation b1*c2-b2*c1=0 is impossible over the
generic RHS field, as predicted by the theorem.

4. A linear normalization is an invalid shortcut
------------------------------------------------
Adding c1*v1+c2*v2=d to that same example leaves the singular solution

  x=0, y=b2/b1, v1=b1, v2=(d-c1*b1)/c2.

For generic coefficients this augmented quotient is finite, yet D=0.
The linear normalization has removed the diagnostic kernel fiber.  It
can also discard regular roots: for f=(x,y), J is the identity, v=b is
forced, and a generic equation c^T v=d simply rejects the regular point.
Allowing d as a new free coordinate only adds a graph and restores no
exclusion.  No such normalization is used in the emitted inputs.

5. Application to the actual cubic contact systems
---------------------------------------------------
The source has n=44 cubic equations in 44 core variables.  Each derivative
has degree at most2, so the added Jv-b and Jw-c equations remain cubic.
The six prescribed small-open inverse equations also remain cubic.
Hence the emitted one-RHS input has94 variables/equations; the two-RHS
input has138 variables/equations.  Both the point-scaled and the exact
beta-reparameterized systems are emitted with the same explicit RHSs.

The large residue regularity opens delta9 and D10 remain required.  The
construction deals only with the incidence Jacobian open.  Once an exact
finite quotient is available, the remaining open factors can be evaluated
and localized within that finite algebra; they have not been evaluated here.

Standard algebraic facts used
-----------------------------
The tangent-space dimension bound is the local inequality in the Stacks
Project, Section10.60, following Proposition10.60.9:
  https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00KD
The invertible-Jacobian presentation criterion and description of etale
schemes over fields are summarized in Proposition59.26.2:
  https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03PC
The strict dimension estimate for the bad RHS incidence and the fixed-RHS
kernel-fiber argument are supplied above, rather than attributed to those
references as a pre-existing theorem about this contact system.
