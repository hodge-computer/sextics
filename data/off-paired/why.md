This example begins with the sparse holdout chain, with coefficients \(1/2\), and adds
\[
\frac13\cdot X_0^2\cdot X_1\cdot X_2\cdot Y_0\cdot Y_2.
\]
The target remains \(X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3\), and every mixed term still has \(X/Y\)-bidegree \((4,2)\). The added term breaks the separate paired sign changes in the first two pairs, while preserving their product and the third paired sign change. It is a controlled loss of symmetry rather than a generic dense perturbation.

The [Fermat-cover construction](../../docs/02-explicit-constructions.md) identifies a second, more direct obstruction to reusing the holdout proof. With \(T^6=Y_0^6+Y_2^6\), \(\alpha=Y_0/T\), \(\beta=Y_2/T\), and \(y=Y_1\), the added term becomes
\[
\frac13\cdot\alpha\cdot\beta\cdot X_0^2\cdot X_1\cdot X_2\cdot T^2.
\]
It is not invariant under \(y\leftrightarrow T\). Thus the equation no longer descends through \(S=y^2+T^2\), \(P=yT\) to the weighted quotient used by that construction. Maintaining the same bidegree and central target is insufficient to maintain the transfer.

The [cyclic-six family theorem](../../docs/04-known-locus-and-frontier.md#a-cyclic-six-family-and-complementary-quotients) requires invariance under the action with weights \((5,1,5)\) on \(Y\). The added term has weight four, so the equation does not retain that action. Preserving the two-link bidegree does not preserve the complementary quotients used by the theorem.

This makes the instance a useful comparison with [the cyclic coupling](../cycle-three/why.md): both produce an unmatched \(T^2\) term on this cover, but only the cyclic example retains the full paired-sign group. One can therefore ask whether the obstruction is best addressed through a replacement cover, a different quotient, or a method insensitive to these sign characters. A failed correction to the existing conic does not exclude such alternatives.

The [mixed-biweight deformation](../mixed-biweight/why.md) supplies the complementary comparison: its paired signs survive, but its cover acquires an unmatched fourth power. Its accepted transitive-cubic construction nevertheless supplies a certificate through a different Jacobian family. Together these entries separate symmetry of the physical sextic, symmetry after base change, and the target-density identities needed to return a certificate.

The [cyclic](../cycle-three/why.md) and [transitive](../mixed-biweight/why.md) cubic constructions use different quotients from the chain cover, but still rely on paired signs to write the equation as a cubic in \(X_i^2\) after fixing \(Y_i/X_i\). The offending term here is odd under simultaneous negation of \(X_0,Y_0\), so that substitution does not produce the same three-square cubic. Thus these Jacobian routes do not remove this example's original symmetry obstruction.

The equation retains more symmetry than the surviving paired signs alone: its projective diagonal symmetries form a group \(G_{36}\) of order \(36\) that fixes the residue of \(A=X_0^3Y_0^3\) identically, with \(G_{36}\)-invariant primitive cohomology of Hodge numbers \((0,22,50,22,0)\), and the coordinate exchange \(\sigma_2:X_2\leftrightarrow Y_2\) also preserves every monomial of \(F\), generating a nonabelian projective symmetry group \(G_{72}\) of order \(72\) containing six reflections. Because \(\sigma_2\) fixes \(A\) while negating the residue volume, all six reflections act by \(-1\) on the entire \((0,22,50,22,0)\) sector, so their [reflection quotients](../../docs/03-geometric-support.md#33-reflection-quotients) serve only the complementary invariant sectors. Quotienting instead by the order-six subgroup \(\langle h\rangle\) acting on \((X_2,Y_2)\) gives a double cover of \(\mathbb P(1,1,1,1,2)\) birational to a quadratic base change of a weighted Calabi–Yau threefold pencil whose holomorphic three-form is fixed by the residual order-three symmetry. Unlike [quadric-contact-residual](../quadric-contact-residual/why.md), only a single coordinate-pair exchange survives rather than two commuting exchanges, so the paired-exchange split-quadric theorem of [§3D.8](../../docs/03d-hodge-loci.md#3d8-split-quadric-sections-and-a-paired-exchange-fourfold-family) does not apply directly either.
