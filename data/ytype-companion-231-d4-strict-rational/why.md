Here all four coefficients of the active binary cubic are nonzero:
\[
p(u,w)=u^3+2\cdot u^2\cdot w+3\cdot u\cdot w^2+w^3,
\qquad (u,w)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2).
\]
The other four sixth powers remain separate. This is a useful comparison with [the first Y-type companion](#case/ytype-companion-fiberA-d4-strict-rational), where one mixed coefficient vanishes. An explanation that relies accidentally on that missing term should fail this comparison.

The target is
\[
A=X_0\cdot Y_0^2\cdot Y_1\cdot
\left(X_0^2+\frac97\cdot Y_0^2\right).
\]
It has the same two monomial positions as the first companion but a different ratio. The question is how that ratio follows the changing cubic, and whether the resulting algebra can remain rational rather than requiring an arbitrary choice of an algebraic root.

The specialization \((\mu,\nu,\gamma)=(2,3,1)\) satisfies \(\nu^3=27\cdot\gamma^2\). That relation supplies a rational parameter in the companion-family calculation. Thus the coefficients are structured: the entry tests a family prediction on a second smooth sextic, rather than claiming that every coefficient triple admits the same simple presentation.

This is also a useful example of the difference between a target and its representation. A general construction can establish existence without revealing the smallest or most transparent pole. Studying alternative presentations can expose the cubic factorization underlying a much larger coefficient system. The broader existence question is addressed by [Chapter 3](#technical-information/geometric-support); this fixed instance focuses attention on rationality, the companion coefficient, and the role of the passive \(Y_1\) factor. Those features are worth retaining when attempting extensions beyond one active binary block.
