The active binary cubic is
\[
p(u,w)=u^3+u^2\cdot w+u\cdot w^2+3\cdot w^3,
\qquad (u,w)=(X_0^2,Y_0^2).
\]
The target \(A=X_1\cdot Y_0^5\) is deliberately a single monomial. In the early binary-block calculations, accessible directions frequently appeared as combinations of two monomials. A nonzero companion coefficient can be mathematically significant: solving a bundle does not by itself solve its constituents.

This coefficient triple belongs to the special locus
\[
\gamma\cdot\mu^3+\nu^2\cdot(\mu^2-4\cdot\nu)=0
\]
in the family \(u^3+\mu\cdot u^2\cdot w+\nu\cdot u\cdot w^2+\gamma\cdot w^3\). Here \((\mu,\nu,\gamma)=(1,1,3)\). It was identified through the compatibility equations for the target family, making it a natural place to investigate whether bundling is necessary or merely a feature of a generic construction.

The four spectator sixth powers are part of the available structure, not disposable decoration. A field acting only on \(X_0,Y_0\) has fewer ways to satisfy its divergence condition than one allowed to use those remaining directions. The instance therefore compares a very simple requested class with a potentially less local rational primitive.

The all-target reflection theorem in [Chapter 3](#technical-information/geometric-support) place its existence in a wider setting. Its particular value remains the distinction between a general existence mechanism and a compact formula for a prescribed monomial. Comparing it with [the sparse X-type bundle](#case/xtype-sparse-rational-1m142) also tests whether different specializations merely change coefficients or require genuinely different ways of combining the active block with its spectators.
