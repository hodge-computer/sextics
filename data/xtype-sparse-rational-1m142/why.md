This instance changes Fermat in just one pair of coordinates. In \(u=X_0^2\), \(w=Y_0^2\), its active block is
\[
p(u,w)=u^3+u^2\cdot w-\frac14\cdot u\cdot w^2+2\cdot w^3,
\]
while the other four coordinates retain separate sixth powers. The equation remains highly structured, but scaling either active coordinate alone no longer preserves the pattern needed by the simplest diagonal formula.

The target is a particular direction in the two-dimensional space
\[
X_1\cdot Y_0^3\cdot\langle X_0^2,Y_0^2\rangle.
\]
Its coefficient \(-1/29\) is essential. A construction for a linear combination of two monomials need not give either monomial individually, so the first question is which combinations the geometry of the binary cubic makes accessible. This is a small setting in which to study the target map of a proposed support, rather than merely whether that support exists.

The coefficients were selected from a rational specialization of a companion family. Consequently, this is a test of a repeatable relation between the cubic and its target, rather than an arbitrary rational point expected to work by chance. Comparing it with [the single-monomial X-type instance](#case/xtype-strict-rational-113) distinguishes a compatible bundle from a specialization where its companion coefficient disappears.

The reflection theorem in [Chapter 3](#technical-information/geometric-support) establishes all-target existence on the surrounding family. What makes this particular entry useful is the possibility of retaining sparse data and rational coefficients throughout. It asks how the spectator coordinates can participate in the divergence cancellation without obscuring the active binary geometry. Those are questions about the form of an explicit certificate, even when abstract existence on the surrounding family is known.
