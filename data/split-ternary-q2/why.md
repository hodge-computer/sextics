Here the two ternary sextics \(f\) and \(g\) are individually asymmetric, but the equation retains the exact decomposition \(F=f(X)+g(Y)\). The target is deliberately a \(q=2\) instance: \(A=X_0^4\cdot X_1^3\cdot Y_0^3\cdot Y_1^2\) has degree twelve and block bidegree \((7,5)\). Its rational differential form has a third-order pole along \(F=0\).

**Geometric existence is known**, by the split theorem and elliptic contraction of [Chapter 3A, §§3A.1–3A.3](#technical-information/split-and-k3). The two triple planes \(u^3=f(X)\) and \(v^3=g(Y)\) acquire divisor support for the required characters after taking products with a Fermat elliptic curve. Their Fano companions supply covering curves; contraction along a translated elliptic diagonal then supports the invariant cohomology of the fourfold. The theorem covers every allowed target and every positive pole parameter, including this exact \(q=2\) target.

The [crossed-ternary construction](#case/crossed-ternary-asymmetric) gives an algebraic model for the contraction step: companion primitives, generic elliptic translation, finite residues with an infinity correction, and derivative-compatible descent. That fixed pole-one application does not itself prove this target. Extending it requires a second-normal-residue argument for the third-order pole, including numerator and Jacobian jets and any tangent correction. Those data must be bound to actual derivatives before descent and contraction can establish the physical identity. The instance tests this higher-pole extension of an established geometric mechanism.

The elementary split Euler field does not settle this choice. For a three-variable block, its strict formula requires target degree three in that block, independently of \(q\); the chosen degrees are seven and five. Neither ternary has a coordinate reflection. Increasing the pole order is therefore a substantive part of this test, rather than multiplication of a known degree-six target by \(F\).

For a denominator \(D\), the six numerators have degree \(\deg(D)+7\). A certificate must establish the full divisibility by \(F^3\), including lower-pole terms. Identifying only the leading Jacobian-ring component would not suffice. The normalized systems in [Chapter 5](#technical-information/computation) provide a concrete computational interface once a promising support is found.

The displayed \(3+3\) splitting is the only linear direct-sum decomposition, up to exchanging blocks; the problem cannot be reduced further to binary blocks by a linear coordinate change.
