The [broken-reflection construction](../quadric-contact-broken/why.md) reduces this sextic to a cubic surface using the quadratic coordinates \(S=2(X_0^2+Y_0^2)\), \(T=4X_0Y_0\), and passive ratios. Its Jacobian supplies the factor \(X_0^2-Y_0^2\), which changes sign under \(X_0\leftrightarrow Y_0\). The target served there has precisely that sign. Here the variety is unchanged, but \(A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3\) is invariant under the same exchange. It therefore cannot be obtained as a polynomial density through that particular quotient map. The other visible exchange, \(X_1\leftrightarrow Y_1\), also fixes this target.

The three paired sign changes provide another reduction: fixing \(Y_i/X_i\) makes the equation cubic in the three squares. Paired signs alone, however, are weaker than the hypotheses of the [cyclic and transitive Jacobian constructions](../../docs/02-explicit-constructions.md). Their sixth-root diagonal action with weights \((2,1,0,3,2,1)\) does not preserve this sextic. The exact calculation in [the Jacobian-scope audit](../../scripts/audit-jacobian-scope.py) shows that the generic auxiliary elliptic surface obtained from this paired cubic has \(\chi(\mathcal O)=3\) and Kodaira dimension one. It is not uniruled, so one cannot obtain the needed dominant rational-curve family while keeping those passive parameters fixed.

The [paired-exchange support theorem of Chapter 3D, §3D.8](../../docs/03d-hodge-loci.md#3d8-split-quadric-sections-and-a-paired-exchange-fourfold-family) supports the entire \(\tau\)-invariant fourth cohomology of this fourfold on a divisor. Its hypotheses hold for the protected equation: every monomial is invariant under \(\tau\), the three paired signs \(s_i:(X_i,Y_i)\mapsto(-X_i,-Y_i)\), and the two coordinate exchanges \(\sigma_0:X_0\leftrightarrow Y_0\) and \(\sigma_1:X_1\leftrightarrow Y_1\), and the reduction modulo five is the smooth Fermat sextic. The support-to-certificate theorem of [Chapter 1](../../docs/01-certificates.md) therefore gives the existence of a certificate for this target, and with [§3.5](../../docs/03-geometric-support.md#35-from-divisor-support-to-every-pole-order) for every \(Y\)-odd numerator at every positive pole order.

The theorem separates \(H^4(X,\mathbb Q)^\tau_{\mathrm{prim}}\) into three parts. The paired-sign proposition of [§3B.9](../../docs/03b-weighted-threefolds.md#3b9-paired-signs) supports the part on which the paired signs act nontrivially, and the reflection theorem of [§3.3](../../docs/03-geometric-support.md#33-reflection-quotients), applied to \(\sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_1\), supports the classes fixed by either exchange. The residue of \(A=X_0^3Y_0^3\) lies in the remaining sector, of Hodge type \((33,102,33)\): invariant under the paired signs and anti-invariant under both exchanges. There §3D.8 moves one-cycles on \(X\). They are cut on the quadric sections \((X_0^2-Y_0^2)+s(X_1^2-Y_1^2)=0\), which have six nodes, by algebraic cycles on a second invariant sextic \(X'\) that contains the same section. The invariant Hodge classes of \(X'\) are algebraic because \(X'/\langle\sigma_0\rangle\) is a smooth Fano fourfold. The argument is not constructive: it uses the Hodge conjecture on \(X'\) and a density argument, and it produces neither the cycles nor a denominator. An explicit certificate and a formal proof of the target remain to be supplied.
