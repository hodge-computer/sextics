The [broken-reflection construction](../quadric-contact-broken/why.md) reduces this sextic to a cubic surface using the quadratic coordinates \(S=2(X_0^2+Y_0^2)\), \(T=4X_0Y_0\), and passive ratios. Its Jacobian supplies the factor \(X_0^2-Y_0^2\), which changes sign under \(X_0\leftrightarrow Y_0\). The target served there has precisely that sign. Here the variety is unchanged, but \(A=X_0^3\cdot Y_0^3\) is invariant under the same exchange. It therefore cannot be obtained as a polynomial density through that particular quotient map. The other visible exchange, \(X_1\leftrightarrow Y_1\), also fixes this target.

The three paired sign changes provide another reduction: fixing \(Y_i/X_i\) makes the equation cubic in the three squares. Paired signs alone, however, are weaker than the hypotheses of the [cyclic and transitive Jacobian constructions](../../docs/02-explicit-constructions.md). Their sixth-root diagonal action with weights \((2,1,0,3,2,1)\) does not preserve this sextic. The exact calculation in [the Jacobian-scope audit](../../scripts/audit-jacobian-scope.py) shows that the generic auxiliary elliptic surface obtained from this paired cubic has \(\chi(\mathcal O)=3\) and Kodaira dimension one. It is not uniruled, so one cannot obtain the needed dominant rational-curve family while keeping those passive parameters fixed.

The [paired-exchange support theorem of Chapter 3D, §3D.8](../../docs/03d-hodge-loci.md#3d8-split-quadric-sections-and-a-paired-exchange-fourfold-family) supplies divisor support for the entire \(\tau\)-invariant fourth cohomology of this fourfold. Its hypotheses hold directly for the protected equation: every monomial is invariant under \(\tau\), the three paired signs \(s_i:(X_i,Y_i)\mapsto(-X_i,-Y_i)\), and the two coordinate exchanges \(\sigma_0:X_0\leftrightarrow Y_0\) and \(\sigma_1:X_1\leftrightarrow Y_1\), and reduction modulo five is the smooth Fermat sextic. The support-to-certificate theorem of [Chapter 1](../../docs/01-certificates.md) therefore establishes rational certificate existence for this exact target (and, with [§3.5](../../docs/03-geometric-support.md#35-from-divisor-support-to-every-pole-order), for every \(Y\)-odd numerator at every positive pole order).

The geometric input separates \(H^4(X,\mathbb Q)^\tau_{\mathrm{prim}}\) into three parts: the paired-sign proposition of [§3B.9](../../docs/03b-weighted-threefolds.md#3b9-paired-signs) supports the sector nontrivial on the paired signs, and the reflection theorem of [§3.3](../../docs/03-geometric-support.md#33-reflection-quotients) applied to \(\sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_1\) supports the \(P\)-invariant classes fixed by either exchange. The present target \(A=X_0^3Y_0^3\) has its residue in the remaining sector \(H^4(X,\mathbb Q)[\chi]\), of Hodge type \((33,102,33)\), which is \(P\)-invariant and anti-invariant under both exchanges. Section 3D.8 obtains moving curve families on \(X\) by intersecting algebraic \((2,2)\)-cycles on a second \(H\)-invariant sextic fourfold \(X'=V(F-R_2(P_2^2-s^2Q_2^2))\)—whose reflection quotient \(X'/\langle\sigma_0\rangle\) is a smooth Fano fourfold—with the moving nodal quadric cones \(P_2+sQ_2=0\), verifies the two-form pairing and weight-four Higgs commutant modulo \(109\), and specializes the supported projector \(e_\chi\) across the smooth \(40\)-coefficient family. An explicit rational primitive is a further algebraic realization of that established support.
