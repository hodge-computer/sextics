Use the cyclic three-link sextic from [the degree-six cyclic instance](../cycle-three/why.md), with
\[
q=2,\qquad
A=X_0^2\cdot X_1^2\cdot X_2^3\cdot Y_0^2\cdot Y_1^2\cdot Y_2.
\]
Every coordinate occurs, and the unequal exponents in the final pair distinguish one position in the cycle. Cyclic permutation produces two companion targets on the same hypersurface.

The pole order requires field components of degree seven and divisibility of \(A-2dF(v)+F\operatorname{div}(v)\) by \(F^3\). Multiplying a degree-six certificate by a polynomial contributes derivatives of that multiplier and does not generally give this identity. The [cyclic Jacobian construction](../cycle-three/why.md) establishes a different, second-order-pole identity.

The sextic is invariant under the sixth-root diagonal action with weights \((2,1,0,3,2,1)\). The present numerator has weight seventeen, while the projective volume contributes nine, so the residue has character \(\zeta_6^{17+9}=\zeta_6^2\). Averaging to the invariant quotient therefore erases it. The [paired-sign sector theorem](../../docs/04-known-locus-and-frontier.md#paired-signs-and-a-surface-quotient) supports the invariant sector; its conclusion does not cover this nontrivial character.

The equation has a second order-three symmetry. Put \(\omega=\zeta_6^2\). The sextic is invariant under the paired sign \(s_2:(X_2,Y_2)\mapsto(-X_2,-Y_2)\) and under
\[
\gamma=\operatorname{diag}(\omega^2,\omega^2,1,\omega,1,1).
\]
The numerator is even in \((X_2,Y_2)\) and has \(\gamma\)-weight \(2\cdot2+2\cdot2+1\cdot2\equiv1\), while the projective volume contributes \(\omega^2\). The residue is therefore invariant under both. More precisely, under the full order-\(72\) projective diagonal symmetry group \(G_{72}\) (generated by the paired signs \(s_i\), \(\tau\), and the cyclic conjugates of \(\gamma\)), the residue lies in a six-dimensional \((3,1)\) character sector of Hodge type \((6,26,6)\) (cf. [§3D.5](../../docs/03d-hodge-loci.md#3d5-sextic-fourfolds-with-a-paired-sign-and-an-order-three-symmetry)) and is already completely invariant under the order-\(24\) subgroup \(G_{24}=\langle s_0,s_1,s_2,\tau,\gamma\rangle\).

The unweighted toric quotient \(X/G_{24}\) nevertheless resists the known rational-surface and conic-bundle constructions. Its five-dimensional Newton polytope has minimum lattice width three over \(\mathbb Z^5\)—excluding linear and quadratic toric variables, hence genus-zero toric curve fibrations and [§3B.11](../../docs/03b-weighted-threefolds.md#3b11-moving-lines-on-a-cubic-threefold-and-global-residue-cancellation)-style toric conic bundles—and its ten width-three directions generate no zero-interior three-dimensional surface fibration. On the full quotient \(X/G_{72}\), the width-three directions yield a unique zero-interior surface fibration, recovering the paired-sign rational elliptic surface of [cycle-three](../cycle-three/solution.md); there, however, the present numerator acquires a cube-root factor of the sixth-root parameter \((Y_0/X_0)^6\) and lifts to a non-uniruled elliptic surface with \(\chi(\mathcal O)=3\) (\(p_g=1\) in the target character).

The [theorem of Chapter 3D on a paired sign and an order-three symmetry](../../docs/03d-hodge-loci.md) supplies divisor support for the fourth cohomology invariant under \(s_2\) and \(\gamma\). Its hypotheses hold for the exact protected equation: the two invariances are visible on its nine monomials, and the fourfold is smooth by the maximum-modulus argument recorded with that chapter's example. The support-to-certificate theorem therefore establishes rational certificate existence for this exact target at pole order two. The two cyclic companions are served by the conjugate symmetries.

The geometric input is the invariant third cohomology of a weighted sextic threefold in \(\mathbb P(1,1,1,1,2)\) under an order-three symmetry that moves its holomorphic form. The pencil \(Y_2=\lambda X_2\), divided by the paired sign, has such fibers:
\[
X_0^6+X_1^6+Y_0^6+Y_1^6+(1+\lambda^6)w^3
+\tfrac12\bigl(X_0^3X_1Y_0Y_1+\lambda X_1^3Y_1w+\lambda X_0Y_0w^2\bigr)=0,
\qquad w=X_2^2.
\]
Chapter 3D moves an explicit divisor class on a cubic section of such a threefold along its Hodge locus, proves by two finite calculations that the resulting curves have nonconstant Abel–Jacobi images and that the invariant part has no proper sub-variation, and specializes the resulting supported projector before transferring support to the fourfold. Retaining the surviving paired signs \(s_0,s_1\) alongside \(\gamma\) on these fibers cuts the target threefold sector from \(h^{2,1}=33\) to \(h^{2,1}=18\) and gives a five-dimensional cubic-section space \(S_3^{[s_0=+,s_1=-,\gamma=1]}\) still satisfying \(n-c=5-1-3=1\), though moving curves away from the Fermat seed remains non-explicit. An explicit rational primitive is a further algebraic realization of that established support.

The [holdout \(q=2\) target](../holdout-q2/why.md) belongs to the [cyclic-six family](../../docs/04-known-locus-and-frontier.md#a-cyclic-six-family-and-complementary-quotients), whose action with weights \((0,0,0,5,1,5)\) is broken by the closing coupling here. Thus the three diagonal actions have distinct scopes: the sixth-root action supports its invariant sector, \((s_2,\gamma)\) supports the present character, and the cyclic-six deck/exchange theorem requires a different symmetry.
