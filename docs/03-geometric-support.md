# 3. Geometric support, finite groups and surface fibrations

[Previous: binary-block constructions](#technical-information/binary-blocks) · [Contents](#technical-information/introduction) · [Next: split sextics and K3 correspondences](#technical-information/split-and-k3)

A certificate constructs a primitive on the complement of a divisor. Geometry establishes the same vanishing through Gysin maps and correspondences. A finite quotient serves the image of its averaging projector; the geometry of the quotient determines whether that image has support. This chapter develops the shared tools: quotient transfer, covering rational curves, surface fibrations with \(p_g=q=0\), and supported projectors in families. Its Fano theorem supplies both reflection sectors and the algebraic Hodge classes used in [Chapter 3B](#technical-information/weighted-threefolds); the relative-projector lemma there supports the remaining two-reflection sector. Chapter 4 applies the surface criterion to paired-sign sextics. Work in characteristic zero; cohomology is rational, or algebraic de Rham after scalar extension.

## 3.1 Divisor support and its source

For a smooth projective variety \(X\), a class is divisor-supported if it vanishes on \(X\setminus Z\) for some divisor \(Z\). The resulting subspace is \(N^{1}H^{k}(X)\). Here is the Gysin description of that space. Take an embedded resolution \(r:\widetilde{X}\to X\) whose reduced inverse image \(E=\bigcup E_i\) is a simple normal-crossing divisor. Localization places \(r^{*}\alpha \) in the image of \(H^{k}_E(\widetilde{X})\to H^{k}(\widetilde{X})\). The Mayer–Vietoris spectral sequence for supports and purity give, for an intersection of \(c\) components, the terms \(H^{k-c-1}(E_{i_{1}}\cap \cdots \cap E_{i_c})(-c)\) contributing to degree \(k\). Their weights are \(k+c-1\). The \(c=1\) terms have weight \(k\), while all deeper intersections have larger weight. Strictness of mixed-Hodge morphisms therefore makes the image in the pure \(H^{k}(\widetilde{X})\) equal to the image of \(\sum _i H^{k-2}(E_i)(-1)\). Push forward and use \(r_*r^{*}=\operatorname{id}\). This proves that \(N^{1}H^{k}(X)\) is generated by the resulting proper Gysin images from smooth divisors. The localization, purity and strictness inputs are stated in [Chapter 1](#technical-information/certificates).

On a fourfold the (3,1) part must consequently come from \(H^{2,0}(E_i)\). Components contracted into codimension at least two have zero (3,1) image: degree-four classes supported in codimension two come from degree-zero classes of surfaces with twist \((-2)\), and hence have type (2,2). Thus resolutions of the actual divisor components supply the relevant holomorphic two-forms. A count of singularities or contact multiplicities does not determine their Gysin map.

On a smooth sextic fourfold this forces singularities on any divisor supporting a nonzero (3,1) class. Lefschetz gives \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb Z[\mathcal O_X(1)]\), so every nonzero effective divisor \(D\) is ample. The sequence \(0\to\mathcal O_X(-D)\to\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_D\to0\), the vanishing \(H^2(X,\mathcal O_X)=0\), and Kodaira vanishing with Serre duality give \(H^2(D,\mathcal O_D)=0\). If \(D\) is normal with rational singularities, a resolution \(\widetilde D\to D\) has the same coherent cohomology, hence \(H^{2,0}(\widetilde D)=0\). Such a component has zero (3,1) Gysin image. Therefore a support for a nonzero (3,1) class must contain a component which is nonnormal or has nonrational singularities. In particular, replacing a support by a smooth divisor need not preserve its cohomological role.

Support is stable under finite sums: take the union of the supports. For a dominant generically finite proper morphism between smooth varieties of the same dimension, pullback and pushforward preserve divisor support. Pullback uses the inverse image of a support; pushforward uses its proper image, which still has codimension at least one. The degree identity \(p_*p^{*}=\operatorname{deg}(p) \operatorname{id}\) follows from the projection formula.

If a finite group \(G\) acts on a variety \(M\), rational cohomology of the quotient is the invariant cohomology. One proof uses the finite quotient map \(\pi \): the invariant part of \(\pi _*\mathbb Q\) is the constant sheaf \(\mathbb Q\), higher direct images vanish, and taking \(G\)-invariants is exact over \(\mathbb Q\). When \(M\) and the quotient are smooth, the corresponding identities are

\[
\begin{gathered}
\pi _*\pi^{*} = |G| \operatorname{id},             \pi^{*}\pi _* = \sum _{g\in G} g^{*}.
\end{gathered}
\]

They also hold for branched quotients: the top-dimensional cycle of \(M\times _{M/G}M\) is the sum of the graphs of the group elements. Their intersections over the branch locus do not add a top-dimensional component.

**Quotient-transfer lemma.** Let \(M\) be smooth projective with a faithful finite \(G\)-action, and let \(N\) be a smooth projective model of \(M/G\). Then \(H^{k}(N)\) is divisor-supported if and only if \(H^{k}(M)^G\) is divisor-supported.

**Proof.** Resolve the quotient rational map equivariantly as \(r:R\to M\) and \(p:R\to N\). Here \(r\) is a sequence of blowups with smooth centers and \(p\) is generically finite of degree \(|G|\). The blowup decomposition

\[
\begin{gathered}
H^{k}(\operatorname{Bl}_C M) = H^{k}(M) \oplus  \bigoplus _{j=1}^{\operatorname{codim} C-1} H^{k-2\cdot j}(C)(-j)
\end{gathered}
\]

shows that \(\operatorname{ker} r_*\) is supported on the exceptional divisors. For this formula, write the exceptional divisor as \(E=\mathbb P(N_{C/M})\) and \(\xi =c_{1}(\mathcal O_E(1))\). The added summands map by \(\gamma \mapsto i_*(\xi ^{j-1}\pi^{*}\gamma )\). The projective-bundle decomposition of \(H^{*}(E)\), together with the localization comparison across \(\operatorname{Bl}_C M\setminus E\cong M\setminus C\), gives the displayed decomposition; \(r_*r^{*}=\operatorname{id}\) splits off \(H^{k}(M)\). If \(H^{k}(M)^G\) is supported, then \(r_*p^{*}\beta \) is supported for every \(\beta \in H^{k}(N)\). Hence \(r^{*}r_*p^{*}\beta \) is supported, and \(p^{*}\beta \) differs from it by an exceptional class. Push forward and divide by \(|G|\) to support \(\beta \).

Conversely, take \(\alpha \in H^{k}(M)^G\) and assume \(H^{k}(N)\) is supported. The class \(p^{*}p_*r^{*}\alpha \) is supported. Over the dense open where \(p\) is the finite group quotient, transfer identifies it with \(\sum _g g^{*}r^{*}\alpha =|G|r^{*}\alpha \). Their difference is supported on the omitted boundary, which can be included in a divisor. Push forward by \(r\) and divide by \(|G|\). This proves the lemma and explains why the boundary cannot be dropped. The same proof shows invariance of the all-supported assertion under smooth projective birational modification. ∎

These constructions use algebraic graphs, blowups and traces. An abstract Hodge isomorphism alone would not transport a supporting equation.

### Averaging projectors and the part served by a quotient

Let a finite group \(G\) act on a smooth projective variety \(M\), and write
\[
e_K=\frac1{|K|}\sum_{k\in K}k\in\mathbb Q[G]
\]
for a subgroup \(K\). Use \(g\mapsto(g^{-1})^*\) for the left action on cohomology; averaging has the same image under either pullback convention. Suppose smooth projective models of \(M/K_1,\ldots,M/K_s\) have divisor-supported \(H^m\). Form the two-sided ideal
\[
I=\sum_j\mathbb Q[G]e_{K_j}\mathbb Q[G].
\]
Then
\[
I H^m(M)\subseteq N^1H^m(M).
\]
Indeed, quotient transfer supports \(e_{K_j}H^m(M)=H^m(M)^{K_j}\), and \(N^1\) is stable under every group element and under finite sums. This proves the assertion for each product \(a e_{K_j}b\) and hence for \(I\).

Maschke's theorem makes the scope exact at the representation-theoretic level. A simple rational representation \(\rho\) occurs in the ideal \(I\) precisely when \(\rho^{K_j}\ne0\) for at least one \(j\): in its simple algebra factor, \(e_{K_j}\) is nonzero exactly under that condition, and any nonzero element generates the factor as a two-sided ideal. Thus the supported invariant spaces, together with their \(G\)-translates, serve the entire isotypic component of every such \(\rho\). Any projector lying in \(I\) has supported image. This is a criterion for these quotients, not an assertion that the remaining representations lack support from other correspondences.

The same argument applies on a finite auxiliary cover. Resolve a projective model equivariantly, compute the ideal there, and use the proper generically finite map to the original variety. A physical class is served when its pullback lies in the supported image; normalized pushforward then descends the support. Reflections, deck traces and exchanges are instances of this one procedure. Finding more curves on a fixed quotient improves its construction, but cannot add a representation absent from its averaging projector.

## 3.2 Curve and surface fibrations

The two criteria below reduce cohomology over an affine base to classes which vanish off a divisor. Rational curves give vanishing directly. Surface fibers with \(p_g=q=0\) give it after removing finitely many relative divisor classes. Finite trace descends the resulting support in both cases.

### Covering rational curves

**Support theorem.** Let \(W\) be a smooth projective \(n\)-fold, \(n>0\). Suppose an actual morphism \(e:\mathbb P^{1}\times S\to W\) is dominant and generically finite, where \(S\) is smooth integral affine of dimension \(n-1\). Then all \(H^{n}(W)\) is supported on one divisor, obtainable by finite algebraic operations from \(e\).

**Proof.** Put \(V=\mathbb A^{1}\times S\). Homotopy invariance and affine de Rham computation give

\[
\begin{gathered}
H^{n}_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=H^{n}_{\mathrm{dR}}(S)=0.
\end{gathered}
\]

Indeed, on \(S\) the de Rham complex is a complex of global sections ending in degree \(n-1\). Compactify \(\mathbb P^{1}\times S\) projectively and take the closure \(\Gamma \) of the graph of \(e\). Its projection \(p:\Gamma \to W\) is proper and generically finite, and \(\Gamma \) contains \(V\) as an open subset. The boundary \(\Gamma \setminus V\) has dimension at most \(n-1\), so its proper image is a proper closed subset of \(W\).

Remove this image and the positive-dimensional-fiber locus of \(p\). On the remaining open the map is finite. Remove its non-étale locus as well; characteristic zero makes this another proper closed subset. Enlarge the union of the removed sets to a divisor \(E\). Then \(p^{-1}(W\setminus E)\) lies in \(V\) and is finite étale of positive degree \(m\) over \(W\setminus E\). The pullback of each global \(\alpha \in H^{n}(W)\) vanishes on \(V\), hence on this smaller open. Finite étale trace gives \(m\alpha |_{W\setminus E}=0\). Notice that no vanishing of all \(H^{n}(p^{-1}(W\setminus E))\) is asserted or needed. ∎

The proof gives the algorithm: form a projective graph closure, eliminate to compute the boundary images, compute fiber-dimension and branch loci, and choose a homogeneous equation containing their union. A proper closed subset of a projective variety has a nonzero equation in some degree, so the final step terminates. If a curve family is initially unparametrized, split its generic conic by a finite extension and shrink the base before applying the theorem; its universal curve need not originally be a product.

The function-field construction in [§2.7](#technical-information/explicit-constructions/27-creating-a-reflection-on-a-finite-cover) makes a related mechanism algebraic at the level of a residue primitive: a polynomial curve family, an invertible normal matrix, polynomial integration, and finite trace. Its proof keeps track of the source density, which is necessary when transferring only a selected cohomological sector.

### Bounded Fano input

We use two external curve theorems. A smooth Fano \(n\)-fold is covered by rational curves \(C\) with \(-K\cdot C\le n+1\). A normal projective \(n\)-fold with ample \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier \(-K\) is covered by rational curves with \(-K\cdot C\le 2\cdot n\). The latter follows from Miyaoka–Mori's bound \(H\cdot C\le 2\cdot n(H\cdot B)/(-K\cdot B)\) applied to a general complete-intersection curve \(B\) in the smooth locus and \(H\) a Cartier multiple of \(-K\). In characteristic zero, a general member of a covering family on a smooth variety is free. These are [Debarre, Theorems 7.5 and 7.7, Proposition 9.12 and Corollary 9.13](https://perso.imj-prg.fr/wp-content/uploads/debarre-pub/M22.pdf).

Here is how bounded curves supply the precise input required above. Embed the Fano model \(W\) by an explicit very ample Cartier line bundle \(L\), and translate the anticanonical bound into \(L\)-degree. For each allowed degree \(e\), the complete scheme of maps \(\mathbb P^{1}\to W\) is cut out by substituting binary \(e\)-forms into the homogeneous equations of \(W\), together with the basepoint-free condition. There are finitely many components in this bounded union. Since their evaluations cover \(W\), at least one has dominant marked evaluation. Dominance can be tested by elimination, or by a differential rank on smooth loci.

If \(W\) is smooth, take a smooth affine open of such a component. At a point where the curve is nonconstant and marked evaluation is submersive, choose \(n-1\) parameter tangent directions which, with the curve direction, span \(T_W\). An affine linear slice through that point realizes those directions. Shrink its smooth component to obtain an affine \(S\) of dimension \(n-1\) with dominant generically finite \(\mathbb P^{1}\times S\to W\).

If \(W\) is singular, first compute a projective resolution \(\widetilde{W}\to W\). The generic parametrized curve is not contained in the exceptional image, so its rational lift to \(\widetilde{W}\) extends across all of \(\mathbb P^{1}\) by properness. Spread the lift over a smaller parameter open and take the same kind of slice there. The support theorem now applies to \(H^{n}(\widetilde{W})\). Curves passing through singular points downstairs must remain in the bounded search; requiring avoidance or a special weighted-coordinate lift could lose the covering component. The degree bound concerns the search downstairs, not the degree of the lifted curves or of the eventual support.

Over an effectively presented field, irreducible decomposition, extraction of a closed point over a finite extension, resolution and the graph operations make this a finite geometric algorithm. It gives a common support for all middle cohomology. It asserts no practical runtime bound.

### Degree-four Hodge classes

**Corollary.** If a smooth projective variety \(W\) satisfies \(H^4(W)=N^1H^4(W)\), every rational Hodge class in \(H^4(W)\) is algebraic. In particular this holds for a smooth Fano fourfold.

Indeed, the Gysin description in §3.1 gives a surjection of rational Hodge structures

\[
\bigoplus_i H^2(\widetilde D_i,\mathbb Q)(-1)\longrightarrow H^4(W,\mathbb Q)
\]

from resolutions of finitely many supporting divisors. Polarizable rational Hodge structures are semisimple: the polarization-orthogonal complement of the kernel gives a rational Hodge splitting. A rational \((2,2)\) class therefore lifts to rational \((1,1)\) classes on the \(\widetilde D_i\). Lefschetz's \((1,1)\) theorem makes these divisor classes; their Gysin images are algebraic codimension-two classes on \(W\). The Fano case follows from covering rational curves above. This also recovers the degree-four statement of the Conte–Murre theorem for uniruled fourfolds, recalled in [Murre, §§2.3–2.5](https://seminariomatematico.polito.it/rendiconti/71-34/473.pdf).

This implication is useful when varying a containing Fano fourfold: its rational Hodge classes can be represented by surfaces and then restricted to a fixed hypersurface. Chapter 3B uses exactly this consequence of the existing support theorem.

### Surface fibers with algebraic second cohomology

**Surface-fibration theorem.** Let \(W\) be a smooth projective fourfold admitting a dominant rational map to a surface whose geometric generic fiber has a smooth projective model \(T\) with \(p_g(T)=q(T)=0\). Assume the generic fiber is geometrically connected. Then
\[
H^4(W,\mathbb Q)=N^1H^4(W,\mathbb Q).
\]
The fiber need not be rational or uniruled.

**Proof.** Resolve the rational map and shrink its base to a smooth affine surface \(B\). Generic smoothness gives a smooth projective morphism \(f:V\to B\); after further shrinking its fibers have \(p_g=q=0\). Birational modifications and the omitted boundary change the support problem only by divisor-supported classes, as in §3.1.

The fibers have \(H^1=H^3=0\), and all of their rational \(H^2\) has type (1,1). Lefschetz's (1,1) theorem therefore makes it algebraic. Fix a relative ample class. Monodromy preserves this class and the integral intersection lattice; the Hodge index theorem makes its orthogonal complement negative definite. The integral isometry group of that complement is finite, so the local system \(R^2f_*\mathbb Q\) becomes constant after a finite étale base change.

Choose a basis of its divisor classes. After a further finite extension of the generic base field these classes are represented by line bundles: the relative Picard scheme has zero-dimensional identity component since \(q=0\), and the obstruction to realizing a Picard class by a line bundle is a Brauer class, split by a finite field extension. Spread finitely many such representatives and remove the branch and indeterminacy loci. We have a finite étale cover of a smaller affine \(B\), with global divisor classes \(d_1,\ldots,d_b\) on \(V\) restricting to a basis on every fiber. A square \(e\) of the relative ample class restricts nontrivially to fiber \(H^4\).

These global cycle classes give a Leray–Hirsch isomorphism
\[
Rf_*\mathbb Q\simeq
\mathbb Q\oplus\mathbb Q(-1)[-2]^b\oplus\mathbb Q(-2)[-4].
\]
For precision, map each shifted constant sheaf by the corresponding global cohomology class. On every stalk the maps give the basis in degrees zero, two and four, and the odd cohomology vanishes; their direct sum is therefore a quasi-isomorphism. In degree four this gives
\[
H^4(V)=H^4(B)\oplus\sum_i d_i\cup f^*H^2(B)
       \oplus e\,H^0(B).
\]
The first term is zero: algebraic de Rham cohomology of a smooth affine surface is computed by its complex of global differential forms, which ends in degree two. Every other term vanishes on the complement of the supports of the chosen divisors, including a divisor representing the ample class. Taking closures gives divisor support in a projective model. Finite étale trace sends this support to the original base; the deleted loci and the boundaries can also be included in a divisor. Pullback and pushforward through the birational modifications finish the proof. ∎

This uses the classical algebraicity of (1,1) classes on surfaces. It does not assume Bloch's conjecture for their zero-cycles, or an algebraic decomposition of their full Chow motives. After finite base change, the degree-four classes are supplied by the second cohomology of the base with a Tate twist and by algebraic fiber classes. The proof establishes existence of divisor support; explicit equations for those divisor families are additional data needed for a computed certificate.

## 3.3 Reflection quotients

**Reflection theorem.** If a smooth sextic \(F\) is even in a coordinate \(z\), every target odd in \(z\) has divisor support in its residue class. Retain the global \(Y\)-odd condition when applying this to our certificate problem. If \(F\) is separately even in \(Y_{0},Y_{1},Y_{2}\), or separately even in \(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2}\), every allowed target at every positive \(q\) has a finite geometric certificate construction.

**Proof for one reflection.** Write \(F(z,x)=H(z^{2},x_{1},\ldots ,x_{5})\). The quotient by \(\sigma :z\mapsto -z\) is

\[
\begin{gathered}
W=\{H(u,x)=0\} \subset  \mathbb P(2,1,1,1,1,1).
\end{gathered}
\]

The coefficient of \(z^{6}\) cannot vanish: otherwise the \(z\)-axis point lies on \(X\) and all derivatives vanish there, because evenness forbids the only potentially relevant monomials \(z^{5}\cdot x_i\). Thus \(W\) misses the sole singular point of its weighted ambient space. On an ordinary chart \(x_i\ne 0\), if \(H\) and all its partials vanished, lifting \(u\) to \(z^{2}\) would make \(F\) and all its partials vanish. Hence \(W\) is smooth. Weighted adjunction gives \(-K_W=\mathcal O_W(1)\), an ample line bundle because \(W\) avoids the weighted singular point. Equivalently, Hurwitz gives \(\pi^{*}(-K_W)=\mathcal O_X(1)\), since \(K_X=0\) and the reflection branch divisor has class \(\mathcal O_X(1)\).

The residue volume changes sign under \(\sigma \). Therefore an odd numerator \(A\) gives a \(\sigma \)-invariant residue \(\alpha _A\), and transfer writes \(\alpha _A=\pi^{*}\beta _A\) with \(\beta _A=\pi _*\alpha _A/2\). By the preceding theorem, covering rational curves on \(W\) compute a divisor supporting \(H^{4}(W)\). Pull it back to \(X\). ∎

The bounded incidence here is particularly small. A degree-\(e\) map is represented by five binary \(e\)-forms and one binary \(2\cdot e\)-form, for \(7\cdot e+6\) coefficients. The equation \(H(\phi )=0\) gives \(6\cdot e+1\) scalar equations; \(e\le 5\) suffices. The five weight-one forms must have no common zero, since \(W\) avoids the weighted vertex. The largest incidence thus has 41 coefficients and 31 equations.

Freeness is an exact rank condition. Pull back the weighted Euler and hypersurface normal sequences and twist by \(\mathcal O(-1)\). Since the Euler kernel is \(\mathcal O(-1)\), whose \(H^{0}\) and \(H^{1}\) vanish, the condition \(H^{1}(\phi^{*}T_W(-1))=0\) is equivalent to surjectivity of

\[
\begin{gathered}
H^{0}(\mathcal O(e-1)^{5} \oplus  \mathcal O(2\cdot e-1)) \to  H^{0}(\mathcal O(6\cdot e-1)).
\end{gathered}
\]

The map multiplies by the six partials of \(H\) evaluated at \(\phi \); its matrix is \(6\cdot e\times 7\cdot e\). The splitting theorem for vector bundles on \(\mathbb P^{1}\) identifies this vanishing with nonnegative splitting degrees of \(\phi^{*}T_W\). At a free point the untwisted incidence derivative has rank \(6\cdot e+1\), so the incidence is smooth of dimension \(e+5\). Choose \(e+2\) affine slices with the evaluation rank condition to leave the required three-dimensional \(S\). These slices choose a covering family on the given \(W\); they restrict neither \(F\) nor \(A\).

For three individual \(Y\) reflections, decompose an odd \(A\) into their simultaneous polynomial characters. Every character has at least one odd \(Y\)-coordinate, so each summand is served by one of the three quotients. Add the fields and take the union of the pulled-back supports. The \(X\) version follows because \(A\) has even total degree and hence odd \(X\)-parity as well. The construction commutes with block-linear changes of coordinates. All-\(q\) numerator extraction is proved in §3.6 below.

### An algebraic realization by weighted quartics

A reflection quotient can be treated by explicit incidence rather than by importing the geometric uniruledness theorem into a formal proof. The [coupled Fano example](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/pull/150) has fixed coupling \(5/7\). Its integral equation reduces modulo five to

\[
H_0=x_0^6+x_1^6+x_2^6+x_3^6+x_4^6+u^3.
\]

Over \(\mathbb F_{25}\), choose \(j^2=2\). The weighted quartic

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_0&=j(s^4+t^4),&x_1&=(2+j)(s^3t+st^3),&x_2&=js^2t^2,\\
x_3&=j(s^3t-st^3),&x_4&=s^4-t^4,&u&=s^8+s^4t^4+t^8
\end{aligned}
\]

lies on \(H_0=0\) and has no common zero in its five weight-one coordinates. In the chart \(s=1\), vary the coefficients of \(t,\ldots,t^4\) in all six coordinates, keeping the coefficient of \(t^8\) in \(u\) equal to one. The twenty-four positive coefficients of \(H\) give an invertible twenty-four-by-twenty-four incidence matrix. Inverting also the nonzero linear coefficient of \(x_1\) preserves transversality to the endpoint section.

The construction of `fqlx` lifts this square incidence over the integral endpoint ring, with \(5/7\) fixed throughout. Independent endpoint constants and the transverse coordinate prove dominance; finite type and transcendence degree then give a finite extension of the quotient function field. The inverse incidence matrix supplies a polynomial normal. Integration on that family, finite trace, and legal clearing give normalized quotient certificate data, as in §2.7. Pullback through \(u=Y_2^2\) supplies the reflection-odd target.

The distinction between a curve and a serving target remains essential. Even a complete primitive construction on the quotient reaches the density sector of its pullback. A target even in \(Y_2\) has the opposite residue character and does not follow by changing only the quotient source.

### Finite groups generated by reflections

Let a finite linear \(G\) preserving \(F\) be generated by order-two hyperplane reflections, and assume \(\tau \in G\) or \(-\tau \in G\). The projector criterion of §3.1 has an elementary form here. Put \(U=\sum_s H^4(X)^s\), summing over the reflections. It is supported and \(G\)-stable. In \(H^4(X)/U\), every reflection acts by \(-1\), since \(v+s^*v\in H^4(X)^s\); hence \(G\) acts through its determinant character. Both \(\tau\) and \(-\tau\) have determinant \(-1\) in six variables and induce the same projective involution. Their invariant classes therefore lie in \(U\), proving all-target support. Mere normalization of \(G\) by \(\tau\) does not determine this last character and is insufficient. No commutativity assumption is needed.

The global involution has two fixed plane sextic curves, of codimension three in \(X\). Its transverse quotient singularities are \(\frac12(1,1,1)\). It supplies no branch divisor or Fano quotient of the kind used above; the nonuniruledness of this quotient and the resulting obstruction for its natural cubic cover are proved in §4.7.

### Finite quotients beyond reflections

Let \(g\) be a finite-order automorphism of a smooth projective Calabi–Yau variety. At a fixed point, write the tangent eigenvalues as \(\exp(2\pi i a_j)\), with \(0\le a_j<1\). Their sum is the *age* of the action at that point. If one fixed point has \(0<\sum a_j<1\), [Kollár–Larsen, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701466) shows that the quotient by \(g\) is uniruled. A smooth projective resolution is therefore covered by rational curves. Slicing a dominating curve family gives the generically finite family required in §3.2; finite-quotient transfer then supports the \(g\)-invariant middle cohomology on a divisor.

For a smooth sextic fourfold, adjunction gives trivial canonical bundle, and Lefschetz gives simple connectivity and \(b_2=1\). The latter excludes a product of positive-dimensional projective varieties. Under these additional hypotheses [Kollár–Larsen, Corollary 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701466) strengthens the conclusion to rational connectedness. The support argument only needs uniruledness. This criterion gives existence; it does not supply the explicit rational curves or a denominator-degree bound.

If a diagonal action fixes \(F\) exactly and has weights \(w_j\) modulo \(n\), a monomial numerator \(Z^e\) has residue character

\[
\sum_j(e_j+1)w_j\pmod n.
\]

The added ones record the volume determinant. Thus invariance of the polynomial numerator alone is not the relevant test. The averaging ideal of §3.1 combines the supported residue spaces and their group translates. The cover construction in §2.7 can reach further characters because its useful reflection acts on an auxiliary variety rather than on the original fourfold.

## 3.4 Nonlinear polynomial Piola maps are finite Fano maps

**Theorem.** Let \(\Phi =(\Phi _{1},\ldots ,\Phi _{6})\) be homogeneous polynomials with degrees \(m_{i}\), put \(M=\sum m_{i}\) and \(\delta =\operatorname{det}(d\Phi )\), and suppose

\[
\begin{gathered}
1\le m_{i}\le 5,       7\le M\le 12,       F=G(\Phi ),       \operatorname{wt}(G)=6,\\
X=\{F=0\} \;\text{smooth}\;.
\end{gathered}
\]

Then \(\Phi \) is finite and \(W=\{G=0\}\subset \mathbb P(m_{1},\ldots ,m_{6})\) is a normal Fano fourfold with quotient singularities. For every \(q\ge 1\) and weighted polynomial \(b\) of weight \(6(q+1)-M\), the residue target \(A=\delta b(\Phi )\) is divisor-supported and has a finite geometric certificate construction. In the involution problem retain the required odd target parity. No spectral derivation or eigencondition on \(b\) is assumed.

### Finiteness and quasismoothness

No variable has weight six, so \(G\) has no constant or ordinary linear term. A nonzero common zero \(z\) of the \(\Phi _{i}\) would satisfy \(F(z)=0\) and \(\nabla F(z)=d\Phi (z)^{T}\nabla G(0)=0\). Thus the \(\Phi _{i}\) have only the origin as common affine zero. They form a homogeneous system of parameters: choose homogeneous representatives of a basis of the finite-dimensional quotient \(k[Z]/(\Phi _{1},\ldots ,\Phi _{6})\), and induct on degree to see that these representatives generate \(k[Z]\) over \(k[\Phi ]\). Consequently the affine map \(\Phi \) is finite and surjective, the six \(\Phi _{i}\) are algebraically independent, and \(\delta \ne 0\) in characteristic zero. The induced projective map \(\pi :X\to W\) is finite and surjective.

If \(y\ne 0\) were a critical zero of \(G\), finite surjectivity would supply \(z\ne 0\) with \(\Phi (z)=y\), and the chain rule would make \(z\) a singular point of \(F\). Hence the cone of \(G\) is smooth away from its vertex: \(W\) is quasismooth.

### The coarse canonical class

For well-formed weights, a quasismooth weighted complete intersection of dimension at least three is well formed unless an equation degree equals an ambient weight. This is the well-formedness theorem recalled in [Sano–Tasin, §1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.15209). Here degree six exceeds every weight. Weighted adjunction therefore gives \(-K_W=\mathcal O_W(M-6)\), ample and \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier. Quasismoothness gives quotient singularities. The weighted adjunction and residue facts used here are classical; see [Dolgachev, §§3.3 and 4.2–4.3](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/idolga/wp-content/uploads/sites/1334/2024/08/weighted82.pdf).

There are two exceptional ambient profiles: (1,2,2,2,2,2) and (2,2,2,2,2,2). Indeed, a non-well-formed six-tuple has some five weights divisible by an integer \(a\ge 2\). Their sum is at least \(5\cdot a\), so \(M\le 12\) forces \(a=2\); with weights at most five, those five must all equal two. The sixth can only be one or two. In the first profile, every monomial of \(G\) is even in the weight-one variable \(t\). Set \(u=t^{2}\); the second Veronese identifies \(W\) with an ordinary cubic \(R(u,t_{1},\ldots ,t_{5})=0\) in \(\mathbb P^{5}\). A nonzero critical zero of \(R\) lifts to one of \(G\), so this cubic is smooth. Regrading the all-two profile gives the same conclusion. In both cases the coarse anticanonical class is six times the old rational hyperplane class, at least \(M-6\). This explicit check avoids confusing the weighted-stack canonical class with the coarse one along a divisorial stabilizer.

### The target is a global residue pullback

Let \(\Omega _z\) be contraction of the six-variable volume form with the ordinary Euler field, and \(\Omega _m\) contraction with \(\sum m_{i}t_{i}\partial _{t_{i}}\). Homogeneity gives \(d\Phi (\epsilon _z)=\epsilon _m\circ \Phi \). Contracting \(\Phi^*(\mathrm dt_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dt_6)=\delta\,\mathrm dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm dz_6\) therefore gives

\[
\begin{gathered}
\Phi^{*}(\Omega _m)=\delta \Omega _z,\\
\Phi^{*}(b\Omega _m/G^{q+1})=\delta b(\Phi )\Omega _z/F^{q+1}.
\end{gathered}
\]

The weight condition makes the downstairs form homogeneous of weight zero and horizontal for the weighted Euler field. It is a global closed form on the complement of \(G=0\) in the smooth weighted projective stack. Residue gives a global \(H^{4}\) class on its smooth hypersurface stack. This may also be constructed on finite quotient charts: ordinary residues commute with the chart group and glue. Rational cohomology of a finite stabilizer vanishes in positive degrees, so the cohomology of the stack equals that of its coarse space. Thus there is a global \(\beta \in H^{4}(W)\) with \(\alpha _A=\pi^{*}\beta \). We have not assumed that an arbitrary class on \(W_{\mathrm{reg}}\) extends across its singularities.

Pull \(\beta \) to a projective resolution \(\widetilde{W}\). The bounded Fano construction supports all \(H^{4}(\widetilde{W})\). Remove from \(W\) the images of that support and the exceptional locus; on the remainder the resolution is an isomorphism and \(\beta \) vanishes. Their inverse images under \(\pi \) are proper closed subsets of \(X\) and can be enlarged to a divisor. This proves the target assertion, including its global extension and boundary terms. ∎

### Why every primitive nonlinear polynomial atom is included

For a \(q=1\) polynomial Piola target \(A=b(\Phi )\delta \) of degree six, homogeneity gives \(\operatorname{wt}(b)=12-M\), so \(6\le M\le 12\). A nonlinear \(\Phi \) has \(M>6\). A weight at least seven forces the profile (7,1,1,1,1,1); then \(G\) ignores that coordinate and \(F\) depends on only five linear forms, contradicting smoothness.

If a weight is six, write \(G=c\cdot t_j+H\), with \(c\) constant. If \(c=0\), the other five positive-degree forms have a common nonzero zero, while their weights are at most two. Since \(H\) has no ordinary linear term, that zero is singular for \(F\). Thus \(c\ne 0\). The polynomial field

\[
\begin{gathered}
B=b(\Phi ) \operatorname{adj}(d\Phi )\cdot e_j/c
\end{gathered}
\]

has degree one and satisfies \(B(F)=A\). Consequently \(A\) lies in the degree-six Jacobian ideal. In the invariant \(q=1\) problem, parity averaging makes \(\operatorname{div}B\) a \(Y\)-odd constant, hence zero. This case is already polynomial and has zero primitive target. Every nonzero primitive nonlinear polynomial atom must therefore satisfy the theorem's degree hypotheses. Linear maps and arbitrary rational adapted maps are not covered by this argument.

Six arbitrary globally \(Y\)-even quadrics composed with any cubic \(G\) illustrate the theorem: \(\delta \) is an odd sextic, and its class pulls back from a smooth cubic fourfold. This is distinct from the cubic-surface construction over passive ratios. More generally, all determinant densities on the same finite map are supported even when a chosen spectral-field constructor serves only a smaller sector. No assertion that those densities exhaust all targets on \(F\) follows.

### A cubic-cover line and its density

For the [cyclic triple-cover equation](#case/cyclic-triple-fano), set \(Z=X_2^3\). The quotient has weights \((1,1,1,1,1,3)\) and admits the line identified by `rbajaj5`,

\[
[X_0:X_1:Z:Y_0:Y_1:Y_2]=[0:s:0:is:t:it].
\]

In the affine chart \(s=1\), use coordinate order \((Y_0,Y_1,X_0,X_1,Y_2,Z)\). A family through an endpoint \((\xi,0,k_0,k_1,k_2,k_3)\) has the form

\[
(\xi+at,\ t,\ k_0+bt,\ k_1,\ k_2+ct,\ k_3+dt+et^2+ft^3).
\]

The constant equation is the endpoint relation; the six positive coefficient equations have Jacobian determinant \(-22500/2401\) at the displayed line. The same incidence, polynomial-normal, dominance and integration argument therefore constructs normalized quotient data for a polynomial source \(B\). The [exact application](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/pull/153) builds on the shared machinery of `fqlx` and `HeadOfNaps`.

Let \(\varphi\) substitute \(Z=X_2^3\). If \((d,N,Q)\) is normalized data for \(B\), put

\[
D=\varphi(d),\qquad
\widetilde N_j=X_2^2\cdot\varphi(N_j)\ (j\ne X_2),\qquad
\widetilde N_{X_2}=\tfrac13\varphi(N_Z).
\]

The chain rule transports the source to \(X_2^2\varphi(B)\) and the reduced divergence numerator to \(F X_2^2\varphi(Q)\). Monic division in an unaffected coordinate proves denominator legality. Thus \(B=X_0Y_0^3\) gives the atlas target \(X_0X_2^2Y_0^3\). A target lacking this density character is not obtained by substituting a different polynomial \(B\). The free line solves the quotient integration problem; it does not remove the character restriction of the finite map.

## 3.5 A self-contained finite bound for the weighted search

For the weights in §3.4 set \(\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(m_1,\ldots,m_6)\). The bundle \(\mathcal O_W(\ell )\) is very ample. The only finite combinatorial ingredient is that the ambient \(\ell \)-Veronese ring is generated in degree one. If a monomial of weight \(k\ell , k\ge 2\), has an exponent \(e_{i}\ge \ell /m_{i}\), remove the pure degree-\(\ell \) factor. Any counterexample therefore has \(0\le e_{i}<\ell /m_{i}\). In this finite box, a bounded subset-sum check shows that a degree-\(\ell \) factor always exists. The following complete integer calculation proves the assertion, the two exceptional profiles and the bound; it has no geometric oracle or omitted lookup table.

```python
from itertools import combinations_with_replacement, product
from math import gcd, lcm

profiles = []
non_well_formed = []
for m in combinations_with_replacement(range(1, 6), 6):
    if not 7 <= sum(m) <= 12:
        continue
    profiles.append(m)
    ell = lcm(*m)
    assert 8*ell // (sum(m)-6) <= 20
    if any(gcd(*(m[:i]+m[i+1:])) > 1 for i in range(6)):
        non_well_formed.append(m)
    for e in product(*(range(ell//a) for a in m)):
        weight = sum(a*b for a, b in zip(m, e))
        if weight < 2*ell or weight % ell:
            continue
        reachable = {0}
        for a, b in zip(m, e):
            reachable = {s+j*a for s in reachable for j in range(b+1)
                         if s+j*a <= ell}
        assert ell in reachable
assert len(profiles) == 26
assert non_well_formed == [(1,2,2,2,2,2), (2,2,2,2,2,2)]
```

Induction now factors every monomial of weight \(k\ell \) into degree-\(\ell \) monomials. Generation gives a closed projective embedding; passing to the hypersurface quotient preserves it. Since \(-K_W\ge (M-6)\mathcal O_W(1)\), the normal-Fano curve bound gives the complete embedding-degree search

\[
\begin{gathered}
L\cdot C \le  \operatorname{floor}(8\ell /(M-6)) \le  20,       L=\mathcal O_W(\ell ).
\end{gathered}
\]

The coarse cubic identifications make the same inequality valid for the two exceptional profiles. The maximum twenty occurs at (1,1,1,1,3,5). This is a bound for curves in a fixed embedding, not a bound for denominator degree. The code is an elementary finite proof of the only profile-specific combinatorial assertion; the geometry is proved above.

## 3.6 From a computed support to every \(q\)

Enlarge a computed proper support in \(X\) to a homogeneous hypersurface \(D=0\) with \(F\nmid D\). If finite coefficient extensions were used, take the union of conjugates; also take the union with its global involution image. The product of the equations gives a rational, globally \(Y\)-even support equation over the original effective field.

For \(1\le q\le 4\), Chapter 1 supplies a numerator over \(\delta =D^q\). Euler normalization makes its extraction the complete linear coefficient system

\[
\begin{gathered}
q\cdot N(F)=A\delta ,\\
\delta  \operatorname{div} N-N(\delta )=F^q\cdot R,\\
\operatorname{deg} N_{i}=q(\operatorname{deg} D+6)-5.
\end{gathered}
\]

All coefficients of the prescribed degrees and parities are included, so the support theorem guarantees consistency. Exact linear algebra therefore finds \(N\). This is a fixed-denominator solve, with no guessed degree search remaining.

For \(q\ge 5\), the regular sequence of six quintic partials has Jacobian-ring socle degree 24. Hence \(A=\sum B_{i}F_{i}\) for homogeneous \(B_{i}\) of degree \(6\cdot q-5\). Average \(B\) to impose parity and put \(c=\operatorname{div} B\). If \(w\) serves \((q-1,c)\), direct differentiation gives

\[
\begin{gathered}
v=(B+F\cdot w)/q,\\
K_q(A;v)=(F/q)K_{q-1}(c;w).
\end{gathered}
\]

This inducts to \(q\le 4\) and adds no denominator support. The full Fano construction thus terminates over effectively presented characteristic-zero fields, using finite-type algebraic operations and resolution. The theorem supplies a finite procedure for legal certificates; it does not promise strictness, a small denominator, or a practical runtime.

## 3.7 Supported projectors in families

The curve and surface arguments above construct support on a total space. A third input is a fiberwise algebraic projector with a representative supported on the square of a divisor. Two lemmas explain how such representatives specialize and spread. Fiberwise coniveau alone does not provide these conclusions: the projector and its support must be parametrized together, and the relative argument below uses concentration in one cohomological degree.

### Specialization

**Supported-projector specialization.** Let \(p:\mathcal X\to B\) be a smooth projective family of \(n\)-folds over a smooth connected complex algebraic base. Let \(P\) be a relative rational algebraic correspondence of codimension \(n\), whose restrictions are projectors. Suppose that, at a very general \(b\), its cohomology class has a representative \(Z_b\) supported on \(D_b\times D_b\), for a divisor \(D_b\subset X_b\). Then such a representative exists at every \(b\in B\).

**Proof.** Write a positive integral multiple of \(Z_b\) as a difference of effective cycles. Parametrize the divisor and these cycles by relative flag Hilbert schemes, imposing the closed incidence conditions that the cycles lie in the divisor's square. There are countably many choices of Hilbert polynomials and integral multiplicities. Each resulting parameter space is proper over \(B\). On each connected component, the equality of the cycle class with the same multiple of \([P_b]\) is locally constant: universal flat cycles give flat sections of the pulled-back cohomology local system of the smooth ambient family.

The component through a representative at a very general point cannot have image contained in a proper closed subset of \(B\). Its image is closed by properness, so it is all of \(B\). The same cohomological identity holds at every point of this component. The fixed Hilbert polynomial of the divisor keeps its support of dimension \(n-1\) after specialization; the flag condition keeps the cycle on its square. Dividing by the chosen integral multiple gives the required rational cycle on every fiber. ∎

The hypothesis asks for equality of cohomology classes, not rational equivalence of projectors. No decomposition of the Chow motive is asserted. Chapter 3B obtains this hypothesis from a curve correspondence and a polynomial inverse on its Hodge-theoretic image.

### Relative support over a curve

**Relative support lemma.** Let \(p:\mathcal T\to U\) be a smooth projective family of threefolds over a smooth complex curve, with a fiberwise algebraic involution \(\sigma\). Suppose

\[
(R^jp_*\mathbb Q)^-=0\quad(j\ne3),
\]

and the anti-projector \(P^-_u=(\Delta_{T_u}-\Gamma_{\sigma_u})/2\) has a cycle representative supported on \(D_u\times D_u\) at every fiber. After removing finitely many points of \(U\), one divisor in \(\mathcal T\) supports \(H^4(\mathcal T,\mathbb Q)^-\). Its closure together with the omitted fibers supports any global class on a smooth projective model whose restriction is anti-invariant.

**Proof.** The flag parameter spaces from the preceding lemma have countably many proper images covering \(U\). One component dominates \(U\). A multisection and normalization give a finite extension of its function field; after shrinking, this is a finite étale cover \(U'\to U\). On the pulled-back family there are a relative divisor \(D'\) and a relative cycle \(Z'\), supported on \(D'\times_{U'}D'\), whose fiber classes equal the anti-projector. Push \(Z'\) along the finite map of relative products and divide by the cover degree. This gives a rational relative cycle \(Z\) over \(U\) with the same fiber classes, supported on \(D\times_U D\), where \(D\) is the finite image of \(D'\). Enlarge \(D\) by its involution image.

Apply \((1-\sigma)/2\) on both factors to \(Z\). This preserves its support and fiber classes and makes its global class anti-invariant in both factors. Let

\[
r:\mathcal T\times_U\mathcal T\longrightarrow U,
\qquad V=(R^3p_*\mathbb Q)^-.
\]

Relative Künneth and the concentration assumption give

\[
(R^kr_*\mathbb Q)^{--}=0\ (k\ne6),\qquad
(R^6r_*\mathbb Q)^{--}=V\otimes V.
\]

The Leray spectral sequence therefore identifies

\[
H^6(\mathcal T\times_U\mathcal T,\mathbb Q)^{--}
\simeq H^0(U,V\otimes V).
\]

In particular, a class in this double-anti-invariant part is determined by its restrictions to the fibers. Both \([Z]\) and the class of the relative anti-projector lie in that part, so their fiberwise equality gives \([Z]=[P^-]\) globally. Their correspondence actions on \(H^4(\mathcal T)\) agree. The action of \(P^-\) is the identity on

\[
H^4(\mathcal T)^-\simeq H^1(U,V),
\]

whereas the action of \(Z\) vanishes after restriction to \(\mathcal T\setminus D\), because its target support is in \(D\). This proves support on the open family. Taking closures and adjoining the boundary divisors proves the last assertion; proper birational pushforward transfers it to another smooth projective model, as in §3.1. ∎

The three fibration inputs now have distinct roles. Rational curves kill cohomology on an affine open. Surface fibers with \(p_g=q=0\) supply global divisor classes and a Leray–Hirsch decomposition after finite base change. The relative support lemma uses a supported projector and a single nonzero anti-invariant cohomology degree. They share the same transfer and boundary arguments, but their hypotheses are not interchangeable.
