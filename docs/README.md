# Sextic fourfolds with an involution

These chapters study the generalized Hodge conjecture for the involution-invariant middle cohomology of a smooth sextic fourfold. The central question is whether that cohomology is supported on a divisor. Rational differential forms turn the support condition into an algebraic certificate equation; correspondences and quotient geometry establish it for several families.

The treatment is organized around general mechanisms and their hypotheses. Examples illustrate those mechanisms, while the case of a general globally \(Y\)-even sextic remains the motivating open problem.

## Reading order

The intended reader knows basic algebraic geometry, differential forms and Hodge decomposition. Chapter 1 identifies the geometric and algebraic formulations. Chapter 2 develops the algebraic calculus of rational primitives, and Chapter 2A works out the relation between binary contacts and Jacobian torsion. Chapters 3–3B establish geometric support theorems and their applications to complete families. Chapter 4 studies representations and deformation, and Chapter 5 develops exact coefficient methods. Foundational results from the literature are cited where they enter.

The Fano quotient theorem supplies the reflection-invariant sectors in Chapter 3B. Families of norm-contact curves on weighted threefolds supply the simultaneous negative sector: their infinitesimal Abel–Jacobi maps are explicit residues, and a finite spanning test gives the required correspondence. Shared support and specialization lemmas extend the result to every smooth member. Binary-block and crossed-split existence follow as corollaries of the two-reflection theorem; Chapter 2A retains the independent contact construction and the general spanning results it reveals.

## Notation

We use \(Z=(X_0,X_1,X_2,Y_0,Y_1,Y_2)\), \(\tau(X,Y)=(X,-Y)\), and \(X=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^5\) for the fourfold. “\(Y\)-even” means even total degree in the three \(Y\) variables, unless individual coordinate reflections are stated. A residue numerator \(A\) is \(Y\)-odd because the projective volume form changes sign under \(\tau\). In the general certificate equation, \(n\) is the ambient projective dimension and \(d=\deg F\); here \(n=5,d=6\). The pole parameter \(q\) is distinct from the denominator degree \(\deg D\).
